Isoplot.McLeanFit — Type
struct McLeanFit{T,N,S}

Result of a maximum-likelihood straight-line fit through data in N dimensions, as returned by mcleanfit. The fitted line is a + v*t, where the first component of v is fixed to 1 (so that the first variable plays the role of x), and has fields

a    :: SVector{N,T}   # A point on the line, with first component equal to the weighted mean of the first variable
v    :: SVector{N,T}   # Direction of the line, with first component equal to one
Σ    :: SMatrix        # Covariance of the 2(N-1) free components of `a` and `v` (excluding the first of each)
mswd :: T              # Reduced chi-squared, with (N-1)(n-2) degrees of freedom

For convenience, the properties slope and intercept (the free components of v, and the point where the first variable is zero) give 1σ Measurements, scalar if N == 2 and SVectors otherwise. In two dimensions the properties xm and ym are also available, giving a point on the line at which the uncertainties in ym and slope are uncorrelated, just as for YorkFit.

Isoplot.UPbAnalysis — Type
struct UPbAnalysis{T} <: NoInitialDaughterAnalysis{T}

Core type for U-Pb analyses. Wraps an Analysis object which has fields

μ :: SVector{T<:AbstractFloat}
σ :: SVector{T<:AbstractFloat}
Σ :: SMatrix{T<:AbstractFloat}

where μ contains the means

μ = [μ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U, μ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U]

where σ contains the standard deviations

σ = [σ²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U, σ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U]

and Σ contains the covariance matrix

Σ = [ σ₇_₅^2  σ₇_₅σ₃_₈
     σ₇_₅σ₃_₈  σ₃_₈^2 ]

If σ is not provided, it will be automatically calculated from Σ, given that σ.^2 = diag(Σ).

Isoplot.chauvenet — Method
chauvenet(μ::AbstractVector, σ::AbstractVector)

Apply Chauvenet's criterion to a set of data to exclude outliers.

The function calculates the z-scores of the data points, and then calculates the probability p of observing a value as extreme as the z-score under the assumption of normal distribution. It then applies Chauvenet's criterion, marking any data point as an outlier if 2 * N * p < 1.0, where N is the total number of data points.

Isoplot.mcleanfit — Method
mcleanfit(x::Collection, σx::Collection, y::Collection, σy::Collection, [ρ]; maxiter=128, tol=1e-12, corrected=false)
mcleanfit(x::Collection{<:Measurement}, y::Collection{<:Measurement}, [ρ]; kwargs...)
mcleanfit(d::Collection{<:AbstractAnalysis}; kwargs...)
mcleanfit(μ::Collection{<:AbstractVector}, Σ::Collection{<:AbstractMatrix}; kwargs...)

Fit a straight line through data in two or more dimensions, with arbitrary correlated uncertainties, following the maximum-likelihood Levenberg-Marquardt approach of McLean (2014), and return a McLeanFit. The first variable is treated as the independent (x) variable.

Data may be given as in yorkfit for two dimensions: x, y, one-sigma uncertainties σx and σy, and optionally correlation coefficients ρ. Alternatively, in any number of dimensions, data may be given as a collection of Analysis objects (or of UPbAnalysis etc.), or as collections of means μ and covariance matrices Σ. Points with any NaNs are ignored.

In two dimensions the fitted line is equivalent to that of yorkfit.

Uncertainties are 1σ. If corrected=true, they are expanded by sqrt(mswd) whenever mswd > 1. The solver stops when the expected improvement in chi-squared from a further Newton step falls below tol, or after maxiter iterations, whichever happens first.

For further reference, see: McLean, N.M. (2014) "Straight line regression through data with correlated uncertainties in two or more dimensions, with an application to kinetic isotope fractionation" Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta 124, 237-249. doi: 10.1016/j.gca.2013.08.035

Examples

julia> x = [0.9304, 2.2969, 2.8047, 3.7933, 5.3853, 6.1995, 6.7479, 8.1856, 8.7423, 10.2588];

julia> y = [0.8742, 2.1626, 3.042, 3.829, 5.0116, 5.5614, 6.7675, 7.8856, 9.6414, 10.4955];

julia> mcleanfit(x, fill(0.25, 10), y, fill(0.25, 10))
McLeanFit{Float64}:
 Maximum-likelihood linear fit of the form y = a + bx with
  intercept: -0.24 ± 0.25 (1σ)
  slope    : 1.043 ± 0.04 (1σ)
  MSWD     : 1.4272439423337353

Isoplot.twanalysis — Method
twanalysis(d::UPbAnalysis)

Convert a U-Pb analysis from Wetherill space (²⁰⁷Pb/²³⁵U, ²⁰⁶Pb/²³⁸U) to Tera-Wasserburg space (²³⁸U/²⁰⁶Pb, ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb), returning a two-dimensional Analysis. Isoplot.yorkfit — Method
yorkfit(x::Collection, σx::Collection, y::Collection, σy::Collection, [ρ]; maxiter=128, tol=1e-12, corrected=false)
yorkfit(x::Collection{<:Measurement}, y::Collection{<:Measurement}, [ρ]; maxiter=128, tol=1e-12, corrected=false)
yorkfit(d::Collection{<:AbstractAnalysis}; maxiter=128, tol=1e-12, corrected=false)

Uses the York (1968) two-dimensional iterative least-squares fit to calculate a, b, and uncertanties σa, σb for the equation y = a + bx, given x, y, uncertaintes σx, and σy, and optionally correlations ρ. Iteration continues until the slope converges to relative tolerance tol, or for at most maxiter iterations, whichever comes first.

If corrected=true, the resulting uncertainties in slope (σb), intercept (σa), and midpoint (σym) are expanded by a factor of sqrt(mswd) to attempt to account for excess dispersion when the MSWD is greater than 1.

For further reference, see: York, Derek (1968) "Least squares fitting of a straight line with correlated errors" Earth and Planetary Science Letters 5, 320-324. doi: 10.1016/S0012-821X(68)80059-7

Examples

julia> x = (1:100) .+ randn.();

julia> y = 2*(1:100) .+ randn.();

julia> yorkfit(x, ones(100), y, ones(100))
YorkFit{Float64}:
 Least-squares linear fit of the form y = a + bx with
  intercept: -0.54 ± 0.45 (1σ)
  slope    : 2.0065 ± 0.0078 (1σ)
  MSWD     : 0.9457955207778813
